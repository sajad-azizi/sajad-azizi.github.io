eMOL electron–molecule scattering

Molecular continuum states, photoionization amplitudes and the observables they reveal.

The computational framework

A molecular potential.
A complex electron wave.

I developed eMOL from scratch to study molecular photoionization and photodetachment. It computes continuum states and transition amplitudes, preserving the phase information needed to study interference, angular distributions and photoemission delays.

The framework combines molecular Hartree–Fock target states and exact exchange with model potentials for correlation and polarization. Prescribed one-, two- and three-dimensional potentials provide controlled tests of the underlying physics.

My contribution. Development of the molecular scattering code, its numerical methods and the analysis tools connecting amplitudes to observables.

01 / Molecular continua

From structure to scattering

Bound and continuum solutions, coupled angular channels, and dipole matrix elements.

02 / Light–matter interactions

One- and two-photon amplitudes

Independently chosen XUV and infrared polarizations, with molecular- and laboratory-frame observables.

03 / Scientific computing

Designed for large calculations

Modern C++ with MPI, OpenMP and SYCL. Multicore, distributed and GPU computing support the molecular calculations.

How to read the checks

Each explorer checks its browser calculations against exported numerical test values. These are implementation consistency checks. Comparisons with independent calculations provide a separate assessment of the physical predictions; their sources and limitations are documented in the explorers.
